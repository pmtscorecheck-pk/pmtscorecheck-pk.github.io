Apna Ghar Apni Chhat and PMT Score — How Housing Eligibility Is Checked in Pakistan

Finding a home of your own is a big step, and for many families in Pakistan, housing schemes are tied to the same welfare checks used for other support. One of those checks is the PMT score. This page explains, in plain words, how that score fits into a housing application like Apna Ghar Apni Chhat, and what you can actually do to check where you stand.

What the PMT score is really looking at

PMT stands for Proxy Means Test. It does not rely on a single payslip. Instead, it builds a picture from household survey data — family size, income sources, the type of house you live in, education and work, and the assets and utilities a household has. The idea is to spot families who are most likely to need help, even when their income is hard to prove on paper. A lower score usually points to greater need.

Why housing schemes ask for it

When a scheme has far more applicants than homes, officials need a fair way to prioritise. The PMT score gives them a common starting point. It does not, by itself, approve or reject a home. It is one part of the record, alongside your CNIC details, survey status and the scheme's own rules. If your household record is incomplete or out of date, the result can look wrong even when your situation has changed.

How to check your status by CNIC

The check most people use in Pakistan runs through the 8171 system. You can open the 8171 web portal, enter your 13-digit CNIC, finish the captcha step, and submit it to see your eligibility result. If you do not have internet access, send your CNIC by SMS to 8171 and wait for the reply. The official helpline, 0800-26477, can help during working hours if you are stuck.

Keep your CNIC number handy, in the usual format, and double-check each digit before you send it. A small typing error is one of the commonest reasons for a "record not found" message.

If your record looks wrong

Do not try to fix it through a random website or agent. The only way to change the official record is through official channels. Visit your nearest BISP office, ask for a household re-survey if your details have changed, and take your CNIC and supporting documents with you. Once the survey is updated, the score can be recalculated. That process takes time, so it is worth doing well before a housing deadline, not after it.

A quick note before you apply

Check your survey status first, keep your family and income details up to date, and use only the 8171 portal, SMS to 8171, or a BISP office for updates. That keeps your application on the official record and avoids delays later.
